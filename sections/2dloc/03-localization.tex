\subsection{Local objects}
\label{subsec:2dloc-local}

\gutentag{0038}%
Fix an $(\infty,2)$-category $\D$ and locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-categories
$I$,
$P \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D)$,
drawn as in \cref{def:adjoin}, a class $Q$ of commutative squares in
$I \cap P$, and let
$\gamma\colon \D \to \D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q}$.
$\D$ may be large (e.g.\ $\D = \Cat$), with small hom-categories;
functor $(\infty,2)$-categories out of $\D$ are then formed in a larger
universe. No other assumption on $\D$ is made in this subsection.
For $s\colon x \to y$ in $\D$ and $d \in \D$ let
\begin{equation*}
s^{\natural} := \Hom_{\D}(s,d)\colon \Hom_{\D}(y,d) \to \Hom_{\D}(x,d) \ ,
\end{equation*}
and for $f\colon d \to d'$ let $f_{\natural}$ be postcomposition.

\begin{defi}
\label{def:IP-local}
\gutentag{0039}%
An object $d \in \D$ is \emph{$(I,P,Q)$-local} if the representable presheaf
$\Hom_{\D}(-,d)$ extends along $\gamma\opcat$,
and a $1$-morphism $f$ of $\D$ is \emph{$(I,P,Q)$-local} if
$f_{\natural} = \Hom_{\D}(-,f)$
does. That is, $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ is the pullback
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Pc_{I,P,Q} \ar[r, "N"] \ar[d, "\iota"'] &
\func\bigl((\D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q})\opcat,\Cat\bigr) \ar[d, "\gamma^{*}"] \\
\D \ar[r, "y"'] & \func(\D\opcat,\Cat)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
of the enriched Yoneda embedding $y$ \cite{hinich2020yoneda,heine2023enriched}.
When $\D$ needs to be named we write $\Pc_{I,P,Q}(\D)$.
For $Q = \emptyset$ we write $\Pc_{I,P}$ and speak of $(I,P)$-local objects
and $1$-morphisms.
\end{defi}

\gutentag{003A}%
We will see in \cref{thm:local-pullback} that $\gamma^{*}$ is a locally full
inclusion. Hence so is $\iota$, and $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ is the locally full
sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of $\D$ on the $(I,P,Q)$-local objects and
$1$-morphisms; and $N$ is fully faithful, as $y$ is.
So $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ is also the full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of
$\func((\D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q})\opcat,\Cat)$
on the presheaves whose restriction along $\gamma$ is representable,
while $\D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q}$ is the one on the representable presheaves;
we will compare the two in \cref{prop:local-vs-free}.
Thus the $(I,P,Q)$-local objects are those whose representable presheaves live
on
the two-dimensional localization, exactly as the $S$-local objects of
\cref{subsec:2dloc-bousfield} are those whose representable presheaves live on
$\C[S^{-1}]$. This is a universal property of $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ for functors into
it:
a functor $\C \to \Pc_{I,P,Q}$ is a functor $G\colon \C \to \D$ for which
$\Hom_{\D}(-,G-)$ extends along
$\gamma\opcat \times \C$.
The definition only uses $\gamma$, so it also applies to the two steps of
\cref{subsec:2dloc-adjoining}: for a locally full
$P' \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D\refl{I})$
we define $\Pc_{I,P'}$ in the same way, with
$\D \to \D\refl{I}\corefl{P'}$
in place of $\gamma$. For $P' = \gamma_{I}(P)$ this is $\Pc_{I,P}$,
and for $P'$ generated by $\gamma_{I}(P)$ and the mirrors of the
$\gamma_{I}(\sigma)$, $\sigma \in Q$, it is $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$
by \cref{rmk:biadjointable}.

\gutentag{003B}%
Restricting $y$ along
$\mathrm{ev}\colon \Delta^{1} \times I \to \D$
and using
$(\Delta^{1})\opcat \simeq \Delta^{1}$
gives a functor
\begin{equation*}
R_{I}\colon \D \longrightarrow \func\bigl(I\opcat,\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)\bigr)
\ , \qquad R_{I}(d)(i) = i^{\natural} \ .
\end{equation*}
It sends a $1$-morphism
$\sigma = (a,b)\colon i \to i'$
of $I$ to the commutative square with sides $i'^{\natural}$, $i^{\natural}$,
$b^{\natural}$, $a^{\natural}$, and $f\colon d \to d'$ to the transformation
with components $f_{\natural}$, whose naturality square at $i$ is filled by the
associativity equivalence
$i^{\natural}f_{\natural} \simeq f_{\natural}i^{\natural}$
(both sides are $f \circ - \circ i$). Likewise we get $R_{P}$,
and restricting $y$ along
$\mathrm{ev}\colon \coprod_{Q}(\Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1}) \to \D$
gives
\begin{equation*}
R_{Q}\colon \D \longrightarrow \prod_{Q}\func(\Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1},\Cat)
\ ,
\end{equation*}
where $R_{Q}(d)(\sigma)$ is the square
$\Hom_{\D}(\sigma,d)$
drawn with the images of the edges in $P$ horizontal,
as we will draw it in \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1).

\begin{thm}
\label{thm:local-pullback}
\gutentag{003C}%
Let
$V\colon \mathcal{T} \to \mathcal{B}$
be the functor
\begin{equation*}
\begin{gathered}
\func\bigl(I\opcat,\func(\Refl,\Cat)\bigr) \times
\func\bigl(P\opcat,\func(\Corefl,\Cat)\bigr) \times
\prod_{Q}\func(\Corefl \times \Refl,\Cat) \\
\xrightarrow{\ (\ell^{*})_{*} \times (\rho^{*})_{*} \times \prod_{Q}(\rho \times
\ell)^{*}\ } \func\bigl(I\opcat,\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)\bigr) \times
\func\bigl(P\opcat,\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)\bigr) \times
\prod_{Q}\func(\Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1},\Cat) \ .
\end{gathered}
\end{equation*}
The square
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:pullback}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Pc_{I,P,Q} \ar[r] \ar[d, "\iota"'] & \mathcal{T} \ar[d, "V"] \\
\D \ar[r, "{(R_{I},R_{P},R_{Q})}"'] & \mathcal{B}
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation}
is a pullback of $(\infty,2)$-categories,
and its vertical maps and $\gamma^{*}$ are locally full inclusions.
The top map sends $d$ to the reflections
$i^{\natural} \dashv (i^{\natural})^{R}$
and the coreflections
$(p^{\natural})^{L} \dashv p^{\natural}$.
Explicitly:
\begin{enumerate}
\item $d \in \D$ is $(I,P,Q)$-local if and only if $i^{\natural}$ has a fully
faithful right adjoint $(i^{\natural})^{R}$ for every object $i$ of $I$,
$p^{\natural}$ has a fully faithful left adjoint $(p^{\natural})^{L}$ for every
object $p$ of $P$, and for all $1$-morphisms
$\sigma = (a,b)\colon (i\colon x \to y) \to (i'\colon x' \to y')$
of $I$ and
$\tau = (a,b)\colon (p\colon x \to y) \to (p'\colon x' \to y')$
of $P$ the commutative squares
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Hom_{\D}(y',d) \ar[r, "b^{\natural}"] \ar[d,
"i'^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(y,d) \ar[d, "i^{\natural}"] \ar[dl, Rightarrow,
shorten=4mm] \\
\Hom_{\D}(x',d) \ar[r, "a^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x,d)
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Hom_{\D}(y',d) \ar[r, "p'^{\natural}"] \ar[d,
"b^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x',d) \ar[d, "a^{\natural}"] \ar[dl, Rightarrow,
shorten=4mm] \\
\Hom_{\D}(y,d) \ar[r, "p^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x,d)
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
filled by $\sigma$, resp.\ $\tau$, are right, resp.\ left, Beck--Chevalley,
i.e.\
$b^{\natural}(i'^{\natural})^{R} \Rightarrow (i^{\natural})^{R}a^{\natural}$
and
$(p^{\natural})^{L}a^{\natural} \Rightarrow b^{\natural}(p'^{\natural})^{L}$
are invertible; and for every $\sigma$ in $Q$ the first square is double
Beck--Chevalley, i.e.\ its double Beck--Chevalley map
\begin{equation*}
(b^{\natural})^{L}(i^{\natural})^{R} \Longrightarrow
(i'^{\natural})^{R}(a^{\natural})^{L}\colon \Hom_{\D}(x,d) \to
\Hom_{\D}(y',d)
\end{equation*}
is invertible.
\item A $1$-morphism $f\colon d \to d'$ between $(I,P,Q)$-local objects is
$(I,P,Q)$-local if and only if for every object $i\colon x \to y$ of $I$ and
every
object $p\colon x \to y$ of $P$ the lax squares
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Hom_{\D}(y,d) \ar[r, "f_{\natural}"] \ar[d,
"i^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(y,d') \ar[d, "i^{\natural}"] \ar[dl, Rightarrow,
shorten=4mm, "="'] \\
\Hom_{\D}(x,d) \ar[r, "f_{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x,d')
\end{tikzcd}
\qquad
\begin{tikzcd}[column sep=large]
\Hom_{\D}(y,d) \ar[r, "p^{\natural}"] \ar[d,
"f_{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x,d) \ar[d, "f_{\natural}"] \ar[dl, Rightarrow,
shorten=4mm, "="'] \\
\Hom_{\D}(y,d') \ar[r, "p^{\natural}"'] & \Hom_{\D}(x,d')
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
(given by associativity
$i^{\natural}f_{\natural} \simeq f_{\natural}i^{\natural}$,
resp.\
$f_{\natural}p^{\natural} \simeq p^{\natural}f_{\natural}$)
are right, resp.\ left, Beck--Chevalley, i.e.\
$f_{\natural}(i^{\natural})^{R} \Rightarrow (i^{\natural})^{R}f_{\natural}$
and
$(p^{\natural})^{L}f_{\natural} \Rightarrow f_{\natural}(p^{\natural})^{L}$
are invertible. In particular $Q$ imposes no condition on $1$-morphisms.
\end{enumerate}
\end{thm}

\gutentag{003D}%
So $d$ is $(I,P,Q)$-local if and only if its representable presheaf takes the
objects of $I$ to left adjoints of reflections and the $1$-morphisms of $I$ to
right Beck--Chevalley squares, and the objects of $P$ to right adjoints of
coreflections and the $1$-morphisms of $P$ to left Beck--Chevalley squares,
and the squares of $Q$, transposed, to double Beck--Chevalley squares;
similarly for $1$-morphisms, with the Beck--Chevalley conditions of
\cref{lem:mates}.
The exchange of $\ell$ and $\rho$, and of the two ways of drawing squares,
reflects the contravariance of $\Hom_{\D}(-,d)$:
the $ (-)\opcat $ involution takes a (co)reflection to a (co)reflection,
but exchanges the roles of the left and right adjoints.
The proof shows more generally that a presheaf $X$ on $\D$ extends along
$\gamma\opcat$ if and only if it satisfies (1) with $X(s)$ in place of
$s^{\natural}$, and that a transformation between such presheaves extends if and
only if it satisfies (2).
By (1) and (2),
$\Pc_{I,P,Q} \subset \Pc_{I,P}$
is the full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category on the objects satisfying the condition
of (1) at the squares of $Q$. Moreover
$\Pc_{I,P} = \Pc_{I,\emptyset} \cap \Pc_{\emptyset,P}$,
and $\Pc_{I,P}$ is the intersection of the
$\Pc_{\{s\},\emptyset}$,
$\Pc_{\{\sigma\},\emptyset}$,
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{t\}}$,
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{\tau\}}$
over the objects $s$ and $1$-morphisms $\sigma$ of $I$ and the objects $t$ and
$1$-morphisms $\tau$ of $P$, where $\{\sigma\}$ denotes the locally full
sub-$(\infty,2)$-category generated by $\sigma$.

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{003E}%
$\func(-,\Cat)$ turns the pushout of \cref{def:adjoin}, applied to $\D\opcat$,
into a pullback: $\gamma^{*}$ is the pullback,
along restriction along $\mathrm{ev}\opcat$, of restriction along
$\rho\opcat \times \mathrm{id}$
and
$\ell\opcat \times \mathrm{id}$
and
$\prod_{Q}(\rho \times \ell)\opcat$
from
$\func(I\opcat,\func(\Refl\opcat,\Cat)) \times
\func(P\opcat,\func(\Corefl\opcat,\Cat)) \times
\prod_{Q}\func(\Refl\opcat \times \Corefl\opcat,\Cat)$.
The equivalences
$\Refl\opcat \simeq \Refl$
and
$\Corefl\opcat \simeq \Corefl$
induced by
$\Adj \simeq \Adj\opcat$
(\cref{subsec:prelim-adjunctions}) carry $\rho\opcat$ to $\ell$ and $\ell\opcat$
to $\rho$; so, after exchanging the two factors of
$\Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1}$,
which transposes the squares $\Hom_{\D}(\sigma,d)$,
the latter restriction is $V$.
It is a locally full inclusion by \cref{lem:mates-refl},
\cref{lem:biadjointable}(2) and \cref{subsec:prelim-conventions},
hence so are its pullbacks $\gamma^{*}$ and $\iota$.
As $y$ restricted along $\mathrm{ev}$ is $(R_{I},R_{P},R_{Q})$,
pasting this pullback with the one of \cref{def:IP-local} shows that
\eqref{eq:pullback} is a pullback.

By \cite[Cor.~2.6.6]{abellangagnahaugseng2024straightening},
$d$ lies in the image if and only if $R_{I}(d)$ sends the objects and
$1$-morphisms of $I\opcat$ into the image of $\ell^{*}$ and $R_{P}(d)$ those of
$P\opcat$ into the image of $\rho^{*}$, and every $R_{Q}(d)(\sigma)$ lies in
the image of
$(\rho \times \ell)^{*}$;
and $f$ does if and only if $R_{I}(f)$,
$R_{P}(f)$ and the $R_{Q}(f)(\sigma)$, as functors out of
$\Delta^{1} \times I\opcat$,
$\Delta^{1} \times P\opcat$
and
$\Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1}$,
send $1$-morphisms into these images. As in the proof of
\cref{prop:adjoin-univ}, for $f$ only the naturality squares at the objects of
$I$ and $P$ impose a condition: by \cref{lem:biadjointable}(2), the condition
on $R_{Q}(f)(\sigma)$ is that its naturality squares at the edges of the
square $R_{Q}(d)(\sigma)$ be Beck--Chevalley, and these edges come from objects
of $I$ and $P$. As a locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category is
determined by its objects and $1$-morphisms (\cref{subsec:prelim-conventions}),
it remains to compute these conditions.

By \cref{lem:mates-refl},
$R_{I}(d)(i) = i^{\natural}$
lies in the image of $\ell^{*}$ if and only if $i^{\natural}$ has a right
adjoint $(i^{\natural})^{R}$ with invertible counit, i.e.\ a fully faithful one,
and
$R_{P}(d)(p) = p^{\natural}$
in that of $\rho^{*}$ if and only if $p^{\natural}$ has a fully faithful left
adjoint $(p^{\natural})^{L}$. For
$\sigma = (a,b)\colon i \to i'$,
$R_{I}(d)(\sigma)$ is the $1$-morphism from $u = i'^{\natural}$ to $u' =
i^{\natural}$ in
$\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)$
with components $f_{0} = b^{\natural}$ and $f_{1} = a^{\natural}$;
by \cref{lem:mates-refl}(2) and \cref{lem:mates}(1) it lies in the image of
$\ell^{*}$ if and only if its right mate
$b^{\natural}(i'^{\natural})^{R} \Rightarrow (i^{\natural})^{R}a^{\natural}$
is invertible. Dually, \cref{lem:mates}(2) reads $R_{P}(d)(\tau)$ as the lax
square with $u = p'^{\natural}$ and $u' = p^{\natural}$ horizontal,
which lies in the image of $\rho^{*}$ if and only if its left mate
$(p^{\natural})^{L}a^{\natural} \Rightarrow b^{\natural}(p'^{\natural})^{L}$
is invertible. Finally, for $\sigma$ in $Q$, $R_{Q}(d)(\sigma)$ is the first
square of (1), with $b^{\natural}$ and $a^{\natural}$ horizontal.
Given the previous conditions, its edges are right adjoints of coreflections,
resp.\ left adjoints of reflections, and it is right, hence left,
Beck--Chevalley (\cref{lem:mates-conjugate}).
So by \cref{lem:biadjointable}(2) for $X = \Corefl$ and $X' = \Refl$,
it lies in the image of
$(\rho \times \ell)^{*}$
if and only if the right mate
of its mirror, the displayed double Beck--Chevalley map, is invertible.
This is (1).
Let $f\colon d \to d'$ with $d$, $d'$ local.
The naturality square of $R_{I}(f)$ at $i$ is a $1$-morphism from $u =
i^{\natural}$ to $u' = i^{\natural}$ in
$\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)$
with $f_{0} = f_{\natural}$ on $\Hom_{\D}(y,-)$ and $f_{1} = f_{\natural}$ on
$\Hom_{\D}(x,-)$. By \cref{lem:mates-refl}(2),
\cref{lem:mates}(1) applies and reads it as the lax square with $u$ vertical and
$\varphi\colon u'f_{0} \simeq f_{1}u$,
here the associativity
$i^{\natural}f_{\natural} \simeq f_{\natural}i^{\natural}$:
this is the first square of (2), and it lies in the image of $\ell^{*}$ if and
only if its right mate
$f_{\natural}(i^{\natural})^{R} \Rightarrow (i^{\natural})^{R}f_{\natural}$
is invertible. Dually, \cref{lem:mates}(2) reads the naturality square of
$R_{P}(f)$ at $p$ as the lax square with $u = p^{\natural}$ horizontal and
$\psi\colon f_{1}u \simeq u'f_{0}$,
here
$f_{\natural}p^{\natural} \simeq p^{\natural}f_{\natural}$:
the second square of (2), which lies in the image of $\rho^{*}$ if and only if
its left mate
$(p^{\natural})^{L}f_{\natural} \Rightarrow f_{\natural}(p^{\natural})^{L}$
is invertible.
No choice is involved: a commutative square has one underlying lax square with
$u$ on a given side, and the $2$-morphism is the one given by $R_{I}$ or
$R_{P}$. (The Beck--Chevalley condition depends on this $2$-morphism,
not only on the edges.)
\end{proof}

\gutentag{003F}%
The reason for the Beck--Chevalley conditions on $1$-morphisms:
a lift of the naturality square of $R_{I}(f)$ at $i$ along $\ell^{*}$ is a
$1$-morphism of $\func(\Refl,\Cat)$, i.e.\ a transformation from the reflection
$i^{\natural} \dashv (i^{\natural})^{R}$
at $d$ to the one at $d'$ with components $f_{\natural}$.
Besides its naturality square at $i^{\natural}$,
it has an \emph{invertible} naturality square at $(i^{\natural})^{R}$,
and in $\htwo\Cat$ this square is the right mate of the former
(\cref{rmk:mates-2cat}). Dually for $p$,
with $(p^{\natural})^{L}$ and the left mate.

\gutentag{0040}%
The case
$\D = \Pc_{\emptyset,\{s\}}$,
i.e.\ every object and $1$-morphism is $\{s\}$-local,
characterizes coreflective $1$-morphisms,
and Beck--Chevalley squares between them, representably:

\begin{lem}
\label{lem:repr-refl}
\gutentag{0041}%
A $1$-morphism $s$ of $\D$ is coreflective if and only if
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{s\}} = \D$,
and reflective if and only if
$\Pc_{\{s\},\emptyset} = \D$.
A commutative square $\sigma$ between coreflective $1$-morphisms,
drawn with these vertical, is right Beck--Chevalley if and only if
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{\sigma\}} = \D$;
one between reflective $1$-morphisms, drawn with these horizontal,
is left Beck--Chevalley if and only if
$\Pc_{\{\sigma\},\emptyset} = \D$.
If $\sigma$ has reflective horizontal and coreflective vertical edges and is
left Beck--Chevalley, then it is double Beck--Chevalley if and only if
$\Pc_{I_{\sigma},P_{\sigma},\{\sigma\}} = \D$,
where $I_{\sigma}$ and $P_{\sigma}$ are generated by $\sigma$ read
horizontally, resp.\ vertically.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{0042}%
The enriched Yoneda embedding
$h\colon \D\opcat \to \func(\D,\Cat)$,
$d \mapsto \Hom_{\D}(d,-)$,
is fully faithful \cite{hinich2020yoneda,heine2023enriched}.
It takes an adjunction $l \dashv r$ of $\D$ to
$r^{\natural} \dashv l^{\natural}$
with the same unit and counit, and every adjunction between representables
arises this way, as adjunctions are detected in the homotopy $2$-category
\cite[Thm.~4.3.11]{riehlverity2016adjunctions} and determined by their left
adjoints \cite[Thm.~4.4]{haugseng2021laxmonads}.
So $s$ is coreflective if and only if $s^{\natural}$ is a right adjoint with
invertible unit in $\func(\D,\Cat)$.
By \cite[Cor.~5.2.12]{abellangagnahaugseng2024straightening},
$s^{\natural}$ is a right adjoint if and only if every component
$s^{\natural}\colon \Hom_{\D}(y,d) \to \Hom_{\D}(x,d)$
is one and every transposed naturality square is left Beck--Chevalley;
these squares are the second squares of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(2).
A $2$-morphism of $\func(\D,\Cat)$ is invertible if and only if its components
are, so the unit is invertible if and only if every $s^{\natural}$ has a fully
faithful left adjoint.
Now let
$\sigma = (a,b)\colon p \to p'$
be a commutative square between coreflective $1$-morphisms.
As $h$ preserves adjunctions, units and counits, it takes the right mate
$ap^{R} \Rightarrow p'^{R}b$
of $\sigma$ to the left mate
$(p^{\natural})^{L}a^{\natural} \Rightarrow b^{\natural}(p'^{\natural})^{L}$
of the square $\Hom_{\D}(\sigma,-)$ of $\func(\D,\Cat)$,
and it reflects invertibility, being fully faithful.
The components of this left mate are the $2$-morphisms of
\cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) for $\tau = \sigma$,
so $\sigma$ is right Beck--Chevalley if and only if every object satisfies the
condition of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) at $\sigma$. By the first part,
the other conditions defining
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{\sigma\}}$
hold for all objects and $1$-morphisms. The reflective cases are dual.
For the last claim,
$\Pc_{I_{\sigma},P_{\sigma}} = \D$
by what we have shown,
and $Q = \{\sigma\}$ imposes no condition on $1$-morphisms
(\cref{thm:local-pullback}(2)). The double Beck--Chevalley map $\delta$ of
$\sigma$ is obtained from $\sigma$ by pasting with the units and counits of the
adjunctions at its edges, and $h$ preserves these.
So $h(\delta)$ is the double Beck--Chevalley map of the square
$\Hom_{\D}(\sigma,-)$
obtained by first mirroring along the images of the horizontal edges of
$\sigma$; by \cite[Rmk.~2.2]{cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal} it agrees with
the one obtained by first mirroring along the other pair of edges,
whose components are the maps of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) for $\sigma \in
Q$. As before, $\delta$ is invertible if and only if $h(\delta)$ is,
i.e.\ if and only if all its components are.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{00A6}%
As $\twoCat$ is cartesian closed, it is enriched over itself,
and changing enrichment along the product-preserving functor
$\iota_{1}\colon \twoCat \to \Cat$
\cite{gepnerhaugseng2015enriched} gives the $(\infty,2)$-category of (possibly
large) $(\infty,2)$-categories, functors and natural transformations,
which we also denote $\twoCat$.

\begin{defi}
\label{def:enriched-adjoint}
\gutentag{0043}%
A functor $G\colon \C \to \D$ of $(\infty,2)$-categories \emph{has a left
adjoint} if it is a right adjoint in $\twoCat$ (\cref{def:adjunction}).
\end{defi}

\gutentag{00A7}%
If $F \dashv G$ with unit $\eta$ and counit $\varepsilon$,
then for $y \in \D$ and $q \in \C$ the composite
\begin{equation*}
\Hom_{\C}(Fy,q) \xrightarrow{\ G\ } \Hom_{\D}(GFy,Gq)
\xrightarrow{\ \eta_{y}^{\natural}\ } \Hom_{\D}(y,Gq)
\end{equation*}
is an equivalence, natural in $y$ and $q$ as functors
$\D\opcat \times \C \to \Cat$:
an inverse is
$g \mapsto \varepsilon_{q} \circ F(g)$,
by naturality of $\eta$ and $\varepsilon$ and the triangle identities in
$\htwo\twoCat$.

\gutentag{0044}%
In general
$\iota\colon \Pc_{I,P,Q} \to \D$
can fail to have a left adjoint. But when it does,
we can compare it with formally making $ I $ reflective and $ P $ coreflective.

\begin{prop}
\label{prop:local-adjoint-refl}
\gutentag{0045}%
Suppose
$\iota\colon \Pc_{I,P,Q} \to \D$
has a left adjoint $L$. Then $Li$ is reflective and $L\sigma$ is left
Beck--Chevalley for every object $i$ and $1$-morphism $\sigma$ of $I$,
and $Lp$ is coreflective and $L\tau$ is right Beck--Chevalley for every object
$p$ and $1$-morphism $\tau$ of $P$, and $L\sigma$ is double Beck--Chevalley for
every $\sigma$ in $Q$. Moreover
$(i^{\natural})^{R} \simeq ((Li)^{L})^{\natural}$
and
$(p^{\natural})^{L} \simeq ((Lp)^{R})^{\natural}$
on
$\Hom_{\D}(-,\iota q) \simeq \Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(L-,q)$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{0046}%
The equivalences
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(Ly,q) \simeq \Hom_{\D}(y,\iota q)$
are natural in $y$ and $q$. They identify $(Li)^{\natural}$ at $q$ with
$i^{\natural}$ at $\iota q$, the square
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(L\sigma,q)$
with
$\Hom_{\D}(\sigma,\iota q)$,
and postcomposition by $g\colon q \to q'$ with $(\iota g)_{\natural}$.
So every object and $1$-morphism of $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ is $\{Li\}$- and
$\{L\sigma\}$-local, and $Li$ is reflective and $L\sigma$ left Beck--Chevalley
by \cref{lem:repr-refl}; likewise for $P$ and $Q$.
Under the Yoneda embedding the reflection $(Li)^{L} \dashv Li$ goes to
$(Li)^{\natural} \dashv ((Li)^{L})^{\natural}$,
so
$((Li)^{L})^{\natural}$
is the right adjoint $(i^{\natural})^{R}$ of $i^{\natural}$; likewise for $p$.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{0047}%
By \cref{rmk:mates-2cat}, for $g\colon q \to q'$ in $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ the squares
expressing that $g_{\natural}$ commutes with precomposition by $(Li)^{L}$ and
$(Lp)^{R}$ are the mates of the squares of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(2):
the Beck--Chevalley conditions say that $\iota g$ commutes with precomposition
by the adjoined adjoints.

\subsubsection*{Local objects and the formal localization}

\gutentag{00A8}%
By \cref{prop:local-adjoint-refl,prop:adjoin-univ}, $L$ extends to a functor
$\Phi\colon \D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q} \to \Pc_{I,P,Q}$
with $\Phi\gamma \simeq L$. Unlike in \cref{prop:bousfield-pushout}(1),
$\Phi$ need not be an equivalence. The following describes $\Phi$ in the terms
of \cref{def:IP-local}.

\begin{prop}
\label{prop:local-vs-free}
\gutentag{00A9}%
Suppose $\iota$ has a left adjoint $L$, and let $y'$ be the enriched Yoneda
embedding of $\D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q}$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item
$N \simeq \Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(\Phi-,-)$,
and for every
$x \in \D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q}$
the $1$-morphism $y'(x) \to N\Phi(x)$ classifying $\mathrm{id}_{\Phi x}$
exhibits $N\Phi(x)$ as a reflection of $y'(x)$ into the full
sub-$(\infty,2)$-category $N(\Pc_{I,P,Q})$.
\item $\Phi$ is fully faithful if and only if every presheaf
$\Hom_{\D\lrqrefl{I}{P}{Q}}(\gamma-,x)$
on $\D$ is representable. This holds if $\gamma$ has a right adjoint,
which is then $\iota\Phi$.
\item The essential image of $\Phi$ is the full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category $K
\subset \Pc_{I,P,Q}$ on the objects $Ld$, $d \in \D$.
So $\Phi$ is an equivalence if and only if it is fully faithful and every
$(I,P,Q)$-local object is equivalent to some $Ld$.
\end{enumerate}
Conversely, if every $y'(x)$ has a reflection into $N(\Pc_{I,P,Q})$,
then $\iota$ has a left adjoint.
\todo{The converse uses the pointwise criterion that $G\colon \C \to \D$ has a
left adjoint if every $\Hom_{\D}(d,G-)$ is corepresentable. Find a reference,
e.g.\ in \cite{hinich2020yoneda,heine2023enriched}.}
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{00AA}%
(1) Both $N$ and
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(\Phi-,-)$
restrict along $\gamma$ to
$\Hom_{\D}(-,\iota-) \simeq \Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(L-,-)$,
as functors
$\Pc_{I,P,Q} \to \func(\D\opcat,\Cat)$.
As $\gamma^{*}$ is a monomorphism, so is postcomposition with it,
and the two agree. So, naturally in $q \in \Pc_{I,P,Q}$,
\begin{equation*}
\Hom\bigl(y'(x),Nq\bigr) \simeq (Nq)(x) \simeq \Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(\Phi x,q)
\simeq
\Hom\bigl(N\Phi(x),Nq\bigr)
\end{equation*}
by the Yoneda lemma and as $N$ is fully faithful,
and the composite is restriction along $y'(x) \to N\Phi(x)$.
(2) By (1) and the Yoneda lemma, the component at $x'$ of $y'(x) \to N\Phi(x)$
is
$\Phi\colon \Hom(x',x) \to \Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(\Phi x',\Phi x)$.
As equivalences in functor $(\infty,2)$-categories are detected componentwise
(\cref{rmk:mates-alternative}), $\Phi$ is fully faithful if and only if every
$y'(x) \to N\Phi(x)$ is an equivalence, i.e.\ if and only if every $y'(x)$ lies
in $N(\Pc_{I,P,Q})$, i.e.\ if and only if every
$\gamma^{*}y'(x) = \Hom(\gamma-,x)$
is representable (\cref{def:IP-local}). If $\gamma$ has a right adjoint $G$,
then
$\Hom(\gamma-,x) \simeq \Hom_{\D}(-,Gx)$
by \cref{def:enriched-adjoint} and the paragraph after it; and then
$\Hom_{\D}(-,\iota\Phi x) = \gamma^{*}N\Phi(x) \simeq \gamma^{*}y'(x)$
gives $\iota\Phi \simeq G$ by the Yoneda lemma for $\D$.
(3) $\gamma$ is essentially surjective, being a pushout of functors that are
essentially surjective, the left class of a factorization system
\cite{gepnerhaugseng2015enriched}; and $\Phi\gamma \simeq L$.
For the converse, a reflection $r$ of $y'(\gamma d)$ into $N(\Pc_{I,P,Q})$
satisfies
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,P,Q}}(r,q) \simeq (Nq)(\gamma d) \simeq \Hom_{\D}(d,\iota q)$,
naturally in $q$, and every $x$ is equivalent to some $\gamma d$.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{00AB}%
So there are two obstructions to $\Phi$ being an equivalence. The first,
that the presheaves $\Hom(\gamma-,x)$ need not be representable,
depends on the squares in $I$, $P$ and $Q$, and adding squares can remove it
(\cref{ex:local-vs-pushout}(1)). The second,
that a local object need not be of the form $Ld$,
comes from $T = \iota L$ not being idempotent,
and in \cref{ex:local-vs-pushout}(2) no choice of squares removes it.
In one dimension neither occurs for the class $W$ of $S$-local equivalences,
which is how \cite[5.2.7.12]{htt} reads in these terms.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:local-vs-pushout}
\gutentag{00AC}%
\leavevmode
\begin{enumerate}
\item Let $\D = \Delta^{1}$ and $P = \{e\}$ for $e\colon 0 \to 1$.
The hom-categories of $\D$ are spaces, and a functor of spaces with a fully
faithful adjoint is an equivalence, so
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{e\}} = \{1\}$
and $\iota$ has the left adjoint
$L\colon \Delta^{1} \to \mathrm{pt}$.
But
$\D\corefl{e} \simeq \Corefl$
(\cref{ex:adjoin-basic}), and
$\Phi\colon \Corefl \to \mathrm{pt}$
is not fully faithful:
$\Hom_{\Corefl}(\gamma-,1)$
takes the value
$\Hom_{\Corefl}(1,1) \simeq \Delta^{1}$
at $1$, so it is not representable on $\Delta^{1}$.
Adding a square removes this. Let $P'$ be generated by $e$ and the square
$(e,\mathrm{id}_{1})\colon e \to \mathrm{id}_{1}$,
the naturality square of the unit $\eta$ at $e$.
As all hom-categories into $1$ are points,
$\Pc_{\emptyset,P'} = \Pc_{\emptyset,\{e\}}$.
A functor
$F\colon \Delta^{1} \to \E$
sends the square to a right Beck--Chevalley square if and only if the counit
$F(e)F(e)^{R} \Rightarrow \mathrm{id}$
is invertible, so $F$ extends to $\D\corefl{P'}$ if and only if $F(e)$ is an
equivalence, and
$\Phi\colon \D\corefl{P'} \simeq \mathrm{pt} \to \Pc_{\emptyset,P'}$
is an equivalence.
\item Let $\D = \Cat$ and
$P = \{\emptyset \to \mathrm{pt}\}$,
so that, by \cref{thm:local-pullback}, $\Pc_{\emptyset,P}$ consists of the
$\infty$-categories with an initial object and the functors preserving it;
we will treat general $P = \{u\}$ in \cref{ex:local-cat}.
Here $\iota$ has the left adjoint
$L(d) = d^{\triangleleft}$:
a functor
$d^{\triangleleft} \to Z$
preserves the initial object if and only if it is left Kan extended from $d$, so
$\Hom_{\Pc_{\emptyset,P}}(d^{\triangleleft},Z) \simeq \func(d,Z)$.
The essential image of $L$ consists of the $Z$ whose initial object $0$ has
$\mapp_{Z}(x,0) = \emptyset$
for $x \not\simeq 0$. The category $Z$ in which $0$ is a retract of $1$,
with $i\colon 0 \to 1$, $r\colon 1 \to 0$ and $ri = \mathrm{id}_{0}$,
has $0$ initial but is not in this image.
Moreover $L$ is not a lax epimorphism (\cref{rmk:lax-epi}). A functor
$Z \to d^{\triangleleft}$
preserving the initial object sends $0$ to the cone point, and hence also $1$,
as $r\colon 1 \to 0$ and no other object maps to the cone point; so
$\Hom_{\Pc_{\emptyset,P}}(Z,Ld) \simeq \mathrm{pt}$
for all $d$. But $\mathrm{id}_{Z}$ and the constant functor at $0$ are
non-equivalent objects of
$\Hom_{\Pc_{\emptyset,P}}(Z,Z)$,
so they induce non-equivalent endomorphisms of
$\Hom_{\Pc_{\emptyset,P}}(Z,-)$
in
$\func(\Pc_{\emptyset,P},\Cat)$,
which $L^{*}$ sends to endomorphisms of the terminal functor. So
$L^{*}\colon \func(\Pc_{\emptyset,P},\Cat) \to \func(\Cat,\Cat)$
is not locally fully faithful. As every functor
$\D \to \D\refl{I'}\corefl{P'}$
of \cref{subsec:2dloc-adjoining} is a lax epimorphism,
$\Pc_{\emptyset,P}$ is not equivalent under $\D$ to any of them,
whatever squares $I'$ and $P'$ contain.
\end{enumerate}
\end{exmp}

\gutentag{00AD}%
A universal property of $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$ for functors out of it therefore has to
restrict the functors. We expect the following to be the right restriction.

\begin{conj}
\label{conj:split-realizations}
\gutentag{00AE}%
Suppose $\iota$ has a left adjoint $L$, and let
$K \subset \Pc_{I,P,Q}$
be as in
\cref{prop:local-vs-free}(3). Let $\E$ be an $(\infty,2)$-category with
geometric realizations, i.e.\ colimits of simplicial objects that every
$\Hom_{\E}(-,e)$ turns into limits. Then restriction along
$K \subset \Pc_{I,P,Q}$
is an equivalence from the full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of
$\func(\Pc_{I,P,Q},\E)$
on the functors preserving geometric realizations of
$\iota$-split simplicial objects onto $\func(K,\E)$.

\end{conj}

\begin{rmk}
\label{rmk:split-realizations}
\gutentag{00AF}%
For $Z \in \Pc_{I,P,Q}$ let
$\mathrm{Bar}_{\bullet}(Z) := (L\iota)^{\bullet+1}Z$
be the bar resolution, a simplicial object of $K$ augmented over $Z$ whose image
under $\iota$ is split. For $Z = Ld$ it is split already in $K$,
by the $1$-morphisms $L\eta_{T^{n}d}$, so every functor preserves its
realization $Ld$. So a functor preserving $\iota$-split realizations is
determined by its restriction to $K$, provided
$Z \simeq |\mathrm{Bar}_{\bullet}(Z)|$
in $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$, and the inverse of the restriction should send $G_{0}\colon K

\gutentag{00B0}%
In the presentable case, \cref{thm:2dloc-local} will identify $\Pc_{I,P}$ with
the pushout in presentable $(\infty,2)$-categories instead.

\gutentag{0048}%
Making $I$ reflective and $P$ coreflective commutes,
also on the level of local objects, as soon as the relevant left adjoint exists.

\begin{prop}
\label{prop:2dloc-commute}
\gutentag{0049}%
Suppose
$\iota_{I}\colon \Pc_{I,\emptyset} \to \D$
has a left adjoint $L_{I}$, with extension
$\Phi_{I}\colon \D\refl{I} \to \Pc_{I,\emptyset}$.
For every locally full
$P' \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D\refl{I})$,
$\iota_{I}$ restricts to an equivalence from
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\Phi_{I}(P')}(\Pc_{I,\emptyset})$
onto $\Pc_{I,P'}$, where $\Phi_{I}(P')$ is generated by the image of $P'$.
In particular
$\Pc_{\emptyset,L_{I}(P)}(\Pc_{I,\emptyset}) \simeq \Pc_{I,P}$
for
$P \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D)$.
Dually, if
$\iota_{P}\colon \Pc_{\emptyset,P} \to \D$
has a left adjoint $L_{P}$, then $\iota_{P}$ restricts to an equivalence from
$\Pc_{L_{P}(I),\emptyset}(\Pc_{\emptyset,P})$
onto $\Pc_{I,P}$, and likewise for
$I' \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D\corefl{P})$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{004A}%
By \cref{prop:local-vs-free}(1) for $(I,\emptyset)$, the extension of
$\Hom_{\D}(-,\iota_{I}q)$
to $\D\refl{I}$ is
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,\emptyset}}(\Phi_{I}-,q)$,
naturally in
$q \in \Pc_{I,\emptyset}$.
As restriction along $\D \to \D\refl{I}$ is a monomorphism,
$\iota_{I}q$ is $(I,P')$-local if and only if
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,\emptyset}}(\Phi_{I}-,q)$
extends along
$\D\refl{I} \to \D\refl{I}\corefl{P'}$.
By the presheaf form of \cref{thm:local-pullback} (the paragraph after it),
this says that
$\Hom_{\Pc_{I,\emptyset}}(-,q)$
satisfies \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) at the objects and $1$-morphisms of
$\Phi_{I}(P')$, i.e.\ that $q$ is
$(\emptyset,\Phi_{I}(P'))$-local
in $\Pc_{I,\emptyset}$; likewise for $1$-morphisms,
with \cref{thm:local-pullback}(2). For $P' = \gamma_{I}(P)$ we have
$\Phi_{I}(P') = L_{I}(P)$,
as
$\Phi_{I}\gamma_{I} \simeq L_{I}$.
The other case is dual.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{00B7}%
As
$\Pc_{I,P,Q} = \Pc_{I,P'}$
for $P'$ generated by $\gamma_{I}(P)$ and the
mirrors of the $\gamma_{I}(\sigma)$, $\sigma \in Q$,
\cref{prop:2dloc-commute} also applies to $\Pc_{I,P,Q}$.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:local-cat}
\gutentag{004B}%
Let $\D = \Cat$ and $P = \{u\}$ for $u\colon X \to Y$. Then
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{u\}}$
is the locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of $\Cat$ on the $\C$ for which
$u^{\natural}\colon \func(Y,\C) \to \func(X,\C)$
has a fully faithful left adjoint $(u^{\natural})^{L}$,
and the $F\colon \C \to \C'$ for which the commutative square
\begin{equation*}
\begin{tikzcd}
\func(Y,\C) \ar[r, "u^{\natural}"] \ar[d, "F_{\natural}"'] & \func(X,\C) \ar[d,
"F_{\natural}"] \ar[dl, Rightarrow, shorten=3mm, "="'] \\
\func(Y,\C') \ar[r, "u^{\natural}"'] & \func(X,\C')
\end{tikzcd}
\end{equation*}
is left Beck--Chevalley, i.e.\
$(u^{\natural})^{L}F_{\natural} \Rightarrow F_{\natural}(u^{\natural})^{L}$
is invertible.
If $u$ is fully faithful, a pointwise left Kan extension along $u$ is fully
faithful, so every $\C$ with colimits indexed by the comma $\infty$-categories
$X \times_{Y} Y_{/y}$ lies in
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{u\}}$.
But $(u^{\natural})^{L}$ need not be pointwise: for
$u\colon \{1\} \to \Delta^{1}$,
a space $\C$ with two points lies in
$\Pc_{\emptyset,\{u\}}$,
as $u^{\natural}$ is an equivalence, but pointwise left Kan extension along $u$
would require an initial object.
Dually,
$\Pc_{\{u\},\emptyset}$
consists of the $\C$ for which $u^{\natural}$ has a fully faithful right adjoint
$(u^{\natural})^{R}$, and the $F$ with
$F_{\natural}(u^{\natural})^{R} \Rightarrow (u^{\natural})^{R}F_{\natural}$
invertible.
\Cref{thm:2dloc-local} will identify these with $\Cat\corefl{u}$ and
$\Cat\refl{u}$.
\end{exmp}
