\subsection{Plain adjoints}
\label{subsec:2dloc-plain}

Arguably it is more natural to formally add adjoints than to formally make certain maps (co)reflective.
We show here that, when lax pushouts exist, we do not lose generality.

First of all, we define formally adjoining adjoints in the same way as in \cref{def:adjoin}.
Replacing $\Refl$ and $\Corefl$ by $\Adj$ in \cref{def:adjoin} gives the pushout $\A\lradj{I}{P}$, which \emph{freely adjoins left adjoints to $I$ and right adjoints to $P$}. We write $\A\ladj{I} := \A\lradj{I}{\emptyset}$ and $\A\radj{P} := \A\lradj{\emptyset}{P}$.
The proof of \cref{prop:adjoin-univ}, with \cref{lem:mates} in place of \cref{lem:mates-refl}, gives its universal property: that of \cref{prop:adjoin-univ} with ``right adjoint'' in place of ``reflective'' and ``left adjoint'' in place of ``coreflective''.

Let $\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P} \subset \D$ be the locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category on the objects for which every $i^{\natural}$ has a right adjoint and every $p^{\natural}$ a left adjoint, not necessarily fully faithful, and the $1$-morphisms of \cref{def:IP-local}.
The proofs of \cref{thm:local-pullback,lem:repr-refl}, with \cref{lem:mates} in place of \cref{lem:mates-refl}, show that $\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P}$ is the pullback of
\[
\textstyle\prod_{I}\ell^{*} \times \prod_{P}\rho^{*}\colon \prod_{I \sqcup P}\func(\Adj,\Cat) \to \prod_{I \sqcup P}\func(\Delta^{1},\Cat)
\]
along $(R_{s})_{s}$, and that $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,\{s\}} = \D$, and a right adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\{s\},\emptyset} = \D$.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:plain-spans}
For small $\A$ and $P$ all $1$-morphisms, $\A\radj{P}$ is the free $(\infty,2)$-category with right adjoints on $\A$. For $\A$ an $(\infty,1)$-category this is the $(\infty,2)$-category of spans of \cite{haugseng2018iteratedspans,cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal}.

We will show that plain adjoining is a special case of \cref{def:IP-local}, provided certain lax pushouts exist. Why \cref{def:IP-local} is the better-behaved notion will be explained in \cref{subsec:2dloc-saturation}.

\subsubsection*{Reduction to coreflections}

\begin{defi}[cylinders]
\label{constr:cylinder}
Let $s\colon x \to y$ in $\D$. A \emph{cylinder} of $s$ is a cocomma object (lax pushout) of $\mathrm{id}_{x}$ and $s$: an object $\Cyl(s)$ with $1$-morphisms $j_{s}\colon x \to \Cyl(s)$, $q_{s}\colon y \to \Cyl(s)$ and a $2$-morphism $\theta\colon j_{s} \Rightarrow q_{s}s$, such that for every $e \in \D$
\[
\Hom_{\D}(\Cyl(s),e) \longrightarrow (\mathrm{id} \downarrow s^{\natural}) \ , \qquad h \mapsto (hj_{s},hq_{s},h\theta) \ ,
\]
is an equivalence onto the comma $\infty$-category of triples $(a,b,a \to s^{\natural}b)$ with $a \in \Hom_{\D}(x,e)$ and $b \in \Hom_{\D}(y,e)$.
Dually, a \emph{cocylinder} of $s$ is a cocomma object of $s$ and $\mathrm{id}_{x}$: an object $\Cyl'(s)$ with $k_{s}\colon x \to \Cyl'(s)$, $q'_{s}\colon y \to \Cyl'(s)$ and $\theta'\colon q'_{s}s \Rightarrow k_{s}$, inducing $\Hom_{\D}(\Cyl'(s),e) \simeq (s^{\natural} \downarrow \mathrm{id})$, the triples $(b,a,s^{\natural}b \to a)$.
\end{defi}

These equivalences are given by precomposition, hence natural in $e$, and under them $j_{s}^{\natural}$ and $k_{s}^{\natural}$ are the projections to $a$. Call a pushout $d = b \sqcup_{a} c$ in $\D$ an \emph{$(\infty,2)$-pushout} if
\[
\Hom_{\D}(d,e) \to \Hom_{\D}(b,e) \times_{\Hom_{\D}(a,e)} \Hom_{\D}(c,e)
\]
is an equivalence for every $e \in \D$. If $\D$ is tensored over $\Cat$, then
\[
\Cyl(s) = (\Delta^{1} \otimes x) \sqcup_{\{1\} \otimes x,\,s} y \ , \qquad \Cyl'(s) = y \sqcup_{s,\,\{0\} \otimes x} (\Delta^{1} \otimes x)
\]
whenever these $(\infty,2)$-pushouts exist. We will see in \cref{subsec:2dloc-presentable} that every presentable $\D$ has all cylinders and cocylinders.

\begin{lem}
\label{lem:comma-adjoint}
Let $R\colon B \to A$ be a functor of $\infty$-categories and $\pi\colon (\mathrm{id}_{A} \downarrow R) \to A$ the projection $(a,b,a \to Rb) \mapsto a$. The following are equivalent:
(a) $R$ is a right adjoint; (b) $\pi$ is a right adjoint; (c) $\pi$ has a fully faithful left adjoint.
If $\Lambda \dashv R$ with unit $\eta$, the left adjoint of $\pi$ is $a \mapsto (a,\Lambda a,\eta_{a})$.
Dually, a functor $G\colon B \to A$ is a left adjoint if and only if the projection $(G \downarrow \mathrm{id}_{A}) \to A$ has a fully faithful right adjoint, given by $a \mapsto (G^{R}a,a,\varepsilon_{a})$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
(a) $\Rightarrow$ (c): $\mapp((a,\Lambda a,\eta_{a}),(a',b',\varphi'))$ is the space of pairs $\alpha\colon a \to a'$, $\beta\colon \Lambda a \to b'$ with $\varphi'\alpha \simeq R(\beta)\eta_{a}$. As $\beta \mapsto R(\beta)\eta_{a}$ is an equivalence $\mapp(\Lambda a,b') \simeq \mapp(a,Rb')$, this space is $\mapp(a,a')$, and the unit is the identity.
(c) $\Rightarrow$ (b) is clear.
(b) $\Rightarrow$ (a): let $\sigma \dashv \pi$ with unit $u$, and $\sigma(a) = (a_{0},b_{0},\varphi\colon a_{0} \to Rb_{0})$. For $b' \in B$, a map $\sigma(a) \to (Rb',b',\mathrm{id})$ is a pair $(R(\beta)\varphi,\beta)$, so $\mapp(\sigma(a),(Rb',b',\mathrm{id})) \simeq \mapp(b_{0},b')$. The adjunction identifies this with $\mapp(a,Rb')$ via $\beta \mapsto R(\beta)\varphi u_{a}$. So $\varphi u_{a}\colon a \to Rb_{0}$ is a universal arrow, and $R$ is a right adjoint.
The dual statement follows by passing to opposite $\infty$-categories.
\end{proof}

So lax pullbacks turn adjunctions into (co)reflections: in the comma square of $R$ and $\mathrm{id}_{A}$, the edge $\pi$ parallel to $R$ is a colocalization exactly when $R$ is a right adjoint, and dually a localization exactly when $G$ is a left adjoint.

\begin{prop}
\label{prop:cylinder}
Let $s\colon x \to y$ be a $1$-morphism of $\D$ with a cylinder $\Cyl(s)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $d \in \D$, $s^{\natural}$ has a left adjoint if and only if $j_{s}^{\natural}$ has a fully faithful left adjoint. For $g\colon d \to d'$ between such objects, the square of \cref{def:IP-local} for $(g,s)$ is left Beck--Chevalley if and only if the one for $(g,j_{s})$ is.
\item $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $j_{s}$ is coreflective.
\end{enumerate}
Dually, if $s$ has a cocylinder, the same holds for right adjoints and $k_{s}$, with $k_{s}$ reflective in (2).
Consequently, if every $p \in P$ has a cylinder and every $i \in I$ a cocylinder, then with $J_{P} := \{j_{p} : p \in P\}$ and $K_{I} := \{k_{i} : i \in I\}$
\[
\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P} = \Pc_{K_{I},J_{P}}
\]
as locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-categories of $\D$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
(1) The first claim is \cref{lem:comma-adjoint} for $R = s^{\natural}$ and \cref{constr:cylinder}. The left adjoint of $j_{s}^{\natural}$ is $\lambda(a) = (a,(s^{\natural})^{L}a,\eta_{a})$, and as the unit of $\lambda \dashv j_{s}^{\natural}$ is the identity, the left mate $\lambda(g_{\natural}a) \to g_{\natural}\lambda(a)$ is the map
\[
\bigl(ga,(s^{\natural})^{L}(ga),\eta_{ga}\bigr) \to \bigl(ga,g(s^{\natural})^{L}a,g\eta_{a}\bigr)
\]
with first component $\mathrm{id}_{ga}$. Its second component $\beta$ satisfies $s^{\natural}(\beta)\eta_{ga} \simeq g\eta_{a}$, so it is the left mate $(s^{\natural})^{L}g_{\natural}a \to g_{\natural}(s^{\natural})^{L}a$ for $s$. A map of $(\mathrm{id} \downarrow s^{\natural})$ is invertible if and only if its components are, so one mate is invertible if and only if the other is.
(2) By \cref{lem:repr-refl} and its plain version, $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,\{s\}} = \D$, and $j_{s}$ is coreflective if and only if $\Pc_{\emptyset,\{j_{s}\}} = \D$. By (1) these conditions agree.
The dual is the same argument with $(s^{\natural} \downarrow \mathrm{id})$. The last claim follows from (1): both sides have the same objects and $1$-morphisms, and a locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category is determined by these.
\end{proof}

So every plain localization is a reflective one, at the cost of replacing $s$ by the inclusion of $x$ into the lax pushout $\Cyl(s)$ or $\Cyl'(s)$. The only hypothesis is the existence of these lax pushouts.
This is the $(\infty,2)$-categorical form of the mapping cone construction of Di Liberti--Lobbia--Sousa \cite[Proposition 1.14]{dilibertilobbiasousa2022kz}: in their terms $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,P}$ is the $2$-category $\mathrm{WLInj}(P)$ of weakly left Kan injective objects and $\Pc_{\emptyset,P}$ is $\mathrm{LInj}(P)$.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:adjoin-products}
Let $\D = \Cat$ and $\nabla\colon \mathrm{pt} \sqcup \mathrm{pt} \to \mathrm{pt}$ the fold map, so that $\nabla^{\natural}\colon \C \to \C \times \C$ is the diagonal. Then $\Cyl(\nabla) = \{a \to c \leftarrow b\}$ and $\Cyl'(\nabla) = \{a \leftarrow c \to b\}$, and $j_{\nabla}$, $k_{\nabla}$ are the inclusions of $\{a,b\}$. Restriction $j_{\nabla}^{\natural}$ has the fully faithful left adjoint $(a,b) \mapsto (a \to a \sqcup b \leftarrow b)$ exactly when $\C$ has binary coproducts.
So $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\{\nabla\},\{\nabla\}} = \Pc_{\{k_{\nabla}\},\{j_{\nabla}\}}$ consists of the $\infty$-categories with binary products and binary coproducts, and the functors preserving both.
\end{exmp}
