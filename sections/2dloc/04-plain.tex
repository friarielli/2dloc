\subsection{Plain adjoints}
\label{subsec:2dloc-plain}

\gutentag{004C}%
Arguably it is more natural to formally add adjoints than to formally make certain maps (co)reflective.
We show here that, when lax pushouts exist, we do not lose generality.

\gutentag{004D}%
First of all, we define formally adjoining adjoints in the same way as in \cref{def:adjoin}.
Replacing $\Refl$ and $\Corefl$ by $\Adj$ in \cref{def:adjoin} gives the pushout $\A\lradj{I}{P}$, which \emph{freely adjoins left adjoints to $I$ and right adjoints to $P$}. We write $\A\ladj{I} := \A\lradj{I}{\emptyset}$ and $\A\radj{P} := \A\lradj{\emptyset}{P}$.
The proof of \cref{prop:adjoin-univ}, with \cref{lem:mates} in place of \cref{lem:mates-refl}, gives its universal property: that of \cref{prop:adjoin-univ} with ``right adjoint'' in place of ``reflective'' and ``left adjoint'' in place of ``coreflective''.

\gutentag{004E}%
Define $\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P}$ as in \cref{def:IP-local}, with $\D \to \D\lradj{I}{P}$ in place of $\gamma$. The proofs of \cref{thm:local-pullback,lem:repr-refl}, with \cref{lem:mates} in place of \cref{lem:mates-refl}, show the following. $\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P}$ is the pullback \eqref{eq:pullback} with $\Adj$ in place of $\Refl$ and $\Corefl$, i.e.\ the locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category of $\D$ described by \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) and (2), except that the adjoints $(i^{\natural})^{R}$ and $(p^{\natural})^{L}$ need not be fully faithful. A $1$-morphism $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,\{s\}} = \D$, and a right adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\{s\},\emptyset} = \D$. A commutative square $\sigma$ between left adjoints, drawn with these vertical, is right Beck--Chevalley if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,\{\sigma\}} = \D$, and one between right adjoints, drawn with these horizontal, is left Beck--Chevalley if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\{\sigma\},\emptyset} = \D$.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:plain-spans}
\gutentag{004F}%
For small $\A$ and $P$ all $1$-morphisms, $\A\radj{P}$ is the free $(\infty,2)$-category with right adjoints on $\A$. Spans need squares as well. Let $\C$ be an $\infty$-category and $I \subset \C$ a wide subcategory closed under pullbacks, and let $I' \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\C\opcat)$ be the locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category whose objects are the $i\opcat$, $i \in I$, and whose $1$-morphisms are the opposites of pullback squares. By the plain version of \cref{prop:adjoin-univ}, $\func(\C\opcat\ladj{I'},\E)$ is the $(\infty,2)$-category of left $I$-adjointable functors $\C\opcat \to \E$ and transformations of Cnossen--Lenz--Linskens \cite[Def.~2.4]{cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal}. So if $I$ is moreover left cancellable, then $\C\opcat\ladj{I'}$ is the $(\infty,2)$-category $\Span_{1\frac{1}{2}}(\C,I)$ of spans whose forward leg is in $I$, by the theorem of Macpherson and Stefanich \cite{macpherson2022bivariant,stefanich2020correspondences} in the form of \cite[Cor.~4.21]{cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal}.
The squares matter already for $\C = I = \Delta^{1}$: the pullback of $e\colon 0 \to 1$ along itself is $\mathrm{id}_{0}$, and the corresponding square of $I'$ is left Beck--Chevalley under $F$ if and only if the unit of $F(e\opcat)^{L} \dashv F(e\opcat)$ is invertible. So $(\Delta^{1})\opcat\ladj{I'} \simeq \Corefl$ by \cref{lem:mates-refl}, while $(\Delta^{1})\opcat\ladj{\{e\opcat\}} \simeq \Adj$.
More generally, the universal $(I,P)$-biadjointable functor $\C\opcat \to \mathbb{B}_{I,P}(\C)$ of \cite[Thm.~2.10]{cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal} is constructed as $\C\opcat\lradj{I'}{P'}$, with $P'$ defined like $I'$ and drawn vertically, followed by a pushout along $\rho \times \ell\colon \Delta^{1} \times \Delta^{1} \to \Adj \times \Adj$ at the pullback squares with horizontal edges in $I$ and vertical edges in $P$, which imposes biadjointability \cite[Prop.~2.12]{cnossenlenzlinskens2025universal}. This is the plain version of \cref{rmk:biadjointable}.
\end{exmp}

\gutentag{0050}%
We will show that plain adjoining is a special case of \cref{def:IP-local}, provided certain lax pushouts exist. Why \cref{def:IP-local} is the better-behaved notion will be explained in \cref{subsec:2dloc-saturation}.

\subsubsection*{Reduction to coreflections}

\begin{defi}[cylinders]
\label{constr:cylinder}
\gutentag{0051}%
Let $s\colon x \to y$ in $\D$. The \emph{cylinder} $\Cyl(s)$ of $s$ is the lax pushout of $\mathrm{id}_{x}$ and $s$, with $j_{s}\colon x \to \Cyl(s)$, $q_{s}\colon y \to \Cyl(s)$ and $\theta\colon j_{s} \Rightarrow q_{s}s$, so that precomposition gives $\Hom_{\D}(\Cyl(s),e) \simeq (\mathrm{id} \downarrow s^{\natural})$, naturally in $e$. Dually, the \emph{cocylinder} $\Cyl'(s)$ is the lax pushout of $s$ and $\mathrm{id}_{x}$, with $k_{s}\colon x \to \Cyl'(s)$, $q'_{s}\colon y \to \Cyl'(s)$, $\theta'\colon q'_{s}s \Rightarrow k_{s}$ and $\Hom_{\D}(\Cyl'(s),e) \simeq (s^{\natural} \downarrow \mathrm{id})$.
\end{defi}

\gutentag{0052}%
Under these equivalences $j_{s}^{\natural}$ and $k_{s}^{\natural}$ are the projections to $\Hom_{\D}(x,e)$. If $\D$ is tensored over $\Cat$, then
\[
\Cyl(s) = (\Delta^{1} \otimes x) \sqcup_{\{1\} \otimes x,\,s} y \ , \qquad \Cyl'(s) = y \sqcup_{s,\,\{0\} \otimes x} (\Delta^{1} \otimes x)
\]
whenever these $(\infty,2)$-pushouts, i.e.\ pushouts taken by every $\Hom_{\D}(-,e)$ to pullbacks, exist. We will see in \cref{subsec:2dloc-presentable} that every presentable $\D$ has all cylinders and cocylinders.

\begin{lem}
\label{lem:comma-adjoint}
\gutentag{0053}%
Let $R\colon B \to A$ be a functor of $\infty$-categories and $\pi\colon (\mathrm{id}_{A} \downarrow R) \to A$ the projection $(a,b,a \to Rb) \mapsto a$. The following are equivalent:
(a) $R$ is a right adjoint; (b) $\pi$ is a right adjoint; (c) $\pi$ has a fully faithful left adjoint.
If $\Lambda \dashv R$ with unit $\eta$, the left adjoint of $\pi$ is $a \mapsto (a,\Lambda a,\eta_{a})$.
Dually, a functor $G\colon B \to A$ is a left adjoint if and only if the projection $(G \downarrow \mathrm{id}_{A}) \to A$ has a fully faithful right adjoint, given by $a \mapsto (G^{R}a,a,\varepsilon_{a})$.
\end{lem}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{0054}%
(a) $\Rightarrow$ (c): $\mapp((a,\Lambda a,\eta_{a}),(a',b',\varphi'))$ is the space of pairs $\alpha\colon a \to a'$, $\beta\colon \Lambda a \to b'$ with $\varphi'\alpha \simeq R(\beta)\eta_{a}$. As $\beta \mapsto R(\beta)\eta_{a}$ is an equivalence $\mapp(\Lambda a,b') \simeq \mapp(a,Rb')$, this space is $\mapp(a,a')$, and the unit is the identity.
(c) $\Rightarrow$ (b) is clear.
(b) $\Rightarrow$ (a): let $\sigma \dashv \pi$ with unit $u$, and $\sigma(a) = (a_{0},b_{0},\varphi\colon a_{0} \to Rb_{0})$. For $b' \in B$, a map $\sigma(a) \to (Rb',b',\mathrm{id})$ is a pair $(R(\beta)\varphi,\beta)$, so $\mapp(\sigma(a),(Rb',b',\mathrm{id})) \simeq \mapp(b_{0},b')$. The adjunction identifies this with $\mapp(a,Rb')$ via $\beta \mapsto R(\beta)\varphi u_{a}$. So $\varphi u_{a}\colon a \to Rb_{0}$ is a universal arrow, and $R$ is a right adjoint.
The dual statement follows by passing to opposite $\infty$-categories.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{0055}%
So lax pullbacks turn adjunctions into (co)reflections: in the comma square of $R$ and $\mathrm{id}_{A}$, the edge $\pi$ parallel to $R$ is a colocalization exactly when $R$ is a right adjoint, and dually a localization exactly when $G$ is a left adjoint.

\begin{prop}
\label{prop:cylinder}
\gutentag{0056}%
Let $s\colon x \to y$ be a $1$-morphism of $\D$ with a cylinder $\Cyl(s)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For $d \in \D$, $s^{\natural}$ has a left adjoint if and only if $j_{s}^{\natural}$ has a fully faithful left adjoint. For $g\colon d \to d'$ between such objects, the second square of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(2) for $(g,s)$ is left Beck--Chevalley if and only if the one for $(g,j_{s})$ is.
Let $\sigma = (a,b)\colon s \to s'$ be a commutative square, where $s'$ also has a cylinder, and let $c_{\sigma}\colon \Cyl(s) \to \Cyl(s')$ correspond to $(j_{s'}a,\,q_{s'}b,\,\theta_{s'}a)$, using $q_{s'}s'a \simeq q_{s'}bs$. Then $\Cyl(\sigma) := (a,c_{\sigma})\colon j_{s} \to j_{s'}$ is a commutative square, and for $d$ such that $s^{\natural}$ and $s'^{\natural}$ have left adjoints, the second square of \cref{thm:local-pullback}(1) for $\tau = \sigma$ is left Beck--Chevalley if and only if the one for $\tau = \Cyl(\sigma)$ is.
\item $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $j_{s}$ is coreflective. If $s$ and $s'$ are left adjoints, then $\sigma$, drawn with $s$ and $s'$ vertical, is right Beck--Chevalley if and only if $\Cyl(\sigma)$ is.
\end{enumerate}
Dually, if $s$ has a cocylinder, the same holds for right adjoints, $k_{s}$ and the analogous square $\Cyl'(\sigma)\colon k_{s} \to k_{s'}$, with $k_{s}$ reflective in (2).
Consequently, suppose that every object of $P$ has a cylinder and every object of $I$ a cocylinder. Let $J_{P} \subset \func(\Delta^{1},\D)$ be the locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-category generated by the $j_{p}$ and the $\Cyl(\tau)$, for the objects $p$ and $1$-morphisms $\tau$ of $P$, and $K_{I}$ the one generated by the $k_{i}$ and the $\Cyl'(\sigma)$ for those of $I$. Then
\[
\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P} = \Pc_{K_{I},J_{P}}
\]
as locally full sub-$(\infty,2)$-categories of $\D$.
\end{prop}

\begin{proof}
\gutentag{0057}%
(1) The first claim is \cref{lem:comma-adjoint} for $R = s^{\natural}$ and \cref{constr:cylinder}. The left adjoint of $j_{s}^{\natural}$ is $\lambda_{s}(u) = (u,(s^{\natural})^{L}u,\eta_{u})$, and as the unit of $\lambda_{s} \dashv j_{s}^{\natural}$ is the identity, the left mate $\lambda_{s}(g_{\natural}u) \to g_{\natural}\lambda_{s}(u)$ is the map
\[
\bigl(gu,(s^{\natural})^{L}(gu),\eta_{gu}\bigr) \to \bigl(gu,g(s^{\natural})^{L}u,g\eta_{u}\bigr)
\]
with first component $\mathrm{id}_{gu}$. Its second component $\beta$ satisfies $s^{\natural}(\beta)\eta_{gu} \simeq g\eta_{u}$, so it is the left mate $(s^{\natural})^{L}g_{\natural}u \to g_{\natural}(s^{\natural})^{L}u$ for $s$. A map of $(\mathrm{id} \downarrow s^{\natural})$ is invertible if and only if its components are, so one mate is invertible if and only if the other is.
For $\sigma$: by construction $c_{\sigma}j_{s} \simeq j_{s'}a$, and precomposition with $c_{\sigma}$ is the functor $(\mathrm{id} \downarrow s'^{\natural}) \to (\mathrm{id} \downarrow s^{\natural})$, $(u',v',\varphi') \mapsto (u'a,v'b,\varphi'a)$, using $v's'a \simeq v'bs$. The same computation, with $a^{\natural}$ and $c_{\sigma}^{\natural}$ in place of the two $g_{\natural}$, shows that the left mate $\lambda_{s}(a^{\natural}u') \to c_{\sigma}^{\natural}\lambda_{s'}(u')$ for $\Cyl(\sigma)$ has first component $\mathrm{id}_{u'a}$ and second component the left mate $(s^{\natural})^{L}a^{\natural}u' \to b^{\natural}(s'^{\natural})^{L}u'$ for $\sigma$.
(2) By \cref{lem:repr-refl} and its plain version, $s$ is a left adjoint if and only if $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,\{s\}} = \D$, and $j_{s}$ is coreflective if and only if $\Pc_{\emptyset,\{j_{s}\}} = \D$; likewise for $\sigma$ and $\Cyl(\sigma)$, whose edges $j_{s}$, $j_{s'}$ are then coreflective. By (1) these conditions agree.
The dual is the same argument with $(s^{\natural} \downarrow \mathrm{id})$. For the last claim, both sides are locally full, so it suffices to compare objects and $1$-morphisms of $\D$. As Beck--Chevalley squares are closed under pasting (\cref{lem:mate-correspondence}(3)), the conditions of \cref{thm:local-pullback} at the generators of $K_{I}$ and $J_{P}$ imply those at all their objects and $1$-morphisms, and by (1) they are the conditions defining $\Pc^{\dashv}_{I,P}$.
\end{proof}

\gutentag{0058}%
So every plain localization is a reflective one, at the cost of replacing $s$ by the inclusion of $x$ into the lax pushout $\Cyl(s)$ or $\Cyl'(s)$, and a square $\sigma$ by $\Cyl(\sigma)$ or $\Cyl'(\sigma)$. The only hypothesis is the existence of these lax pushouts.
This is the $(\infty,2)$-categorical form of the mapping cone construction of Di Liberti--Lobbia--Sousa \cite[Proposition 1.14]{dilibertilobbiasousa2022kz}: in their terms, for a class $P$, $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\emptyset,P}$ is the $2$-category $\mathrm{WLInj}(P)$ of weakly left Kan injective objects and $\Pc_{\emptyset,P}$ is $\mathrm{LInj}(P)$.

\begin{exmp}
\label{ex:adjoin-products}
\gutentag{0059}%
Let $\D = \Cat$ and $\nabla\colon \mathrm{pt} \sqcup \mathrm{pt} \to \mathrm{pt}$ the fold map, so that $\nabla^{\natural}\colon \C \to \C \times \C$ is the diagonal. Then $\Cyl(\nabla) = \{a \to c \leftarrow b\}$ and $\Cyl'(\nabla) = \{a \leftarrow c \to b\}$, and $j_{\nabla}$, $k_{\nabla}$ are the inclusions of $\{a,b\}$. Restriction $j_{\nabla}^{\natural}$ has the fully faithful left adjoint $(a,b) \mapsto (a \to a \sqcup b \leftarrow b)$ exactly when $\C$ has binary coproducts.
So $\Pc^{\dashv}_{\{\nabla\},\{\nabla\}} = \Pc_{\{k_{\nabla}\},\{j_{\nabla}\}}$ consists of the $\infty$-categories with binary products and binary coproducts, and the functors preserving both.
\end{exmp}
